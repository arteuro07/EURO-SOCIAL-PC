# BOSS DE RIEN — Director Guide

This is an original production method inspired by code-driven filmmaking workflows. Do not copy another project's films, style files, prompts, assets, or implementation verbatim.

## Role
The directing system is responsible for story, acting, camera, rhythm, sound, continuity and final quality. A good frame is not enough: the episode must read clearly, feel funny, and hold attention on a phone.

## Episode brief
Default target:
- Platform: TikTok / vertical short video.
- Runtime: about 60 seconds; prototype at 8–12 seconds first.
- Frame: 1080 × 1920, 30 fps for final delivery.
- Language: natural French with selective Gen Z expressions and nouchi; dialogue must remain understandable.
- Publication: manual only. Never auto-post.

## Story rules
1. Hook the viewer in the first 1–3 seconds.
2. One central goal, one conflict, one reversal and one memorable payoff.
3. Give each recurring character a distinct intention and comic rhythm.
4. Keep dialogue short; let acting and visual gags carry the scene.
5. End with a punchline, visual callback or a clear next-episode hook.
6. Write original stories. References are for learning visual grammar, never for copying protected characters, shots, dialogue, music or logos.

## Pre-production
Before animation, write an episode treatment containing:
- logline and comic premise;
- beat sheet with timestamps;
- shot list (framing, angle, camera movement, duration);
- dialogue and performance notes;
- sound cue list;
- continuity notes for characters, props and location.

Create a storyboard/contact sheet using the actual renderer, not placeholder mockups. For a character episode, include front, profile and three-quarter views, key expressions and poses.

## Character performance
Characters must remain on-model between shots. Use a reusable rig and stable proportions, not unrelated drawings generated per frame. Every meaningful action needs anticipation, action and follow-through. Blend poses smoothly; no abrupt pose switching except an intentional comic pop. Expressions must read at final phone size.

## Cinematic direction
Vary wide, medium and close framing. Camera moves must serve the gag or emotion. Use motivated cuts and transitions, readable silhouettes, foreground/background separation, controlled depth, lighting and shadows. Avoid constant motion: pauses make jokes land.

## Sound
Treat dialogue, ambience, foley and music as separate layers. Every important contact or movement should have an intentional sound. Keep music below dialogue. Use only original or properly licensed audio and document every third-party source.

## Review gate
Before an episode is accepted:
- inspect contact sheets and key action strips;
- watch the complete render at normal speed with sound;
- check character identity, anatomy, hand/prop contact, lip timing, subtitles and continuity;
- check for blank frames, clipping, flicker and audio peaks;
- make one focused correction pass and re-check.

Never call a render final until it has been inspected. If a test fails, report the failure and fix the cause instead of hiding it.
